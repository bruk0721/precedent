The workflow this replaces

Today, without this: a casualty underwriter doing a quarterly PFAS-exposed account review manually re-checks state legislation trackers, searches PACER or news coverage for new filings, and cross-references that against a spreadsheet of which accounts sit in which states — usually a half-day of manual research per review cycle, done by hand, from scratch, every quarter.

What Precedent does instead: one evidence-tiered view of litigation, legislation, and prediction, queried directly against your book of business, so the quarterly review becomes "open the tool, paste in this quarter's renewals, read the flagged accounts" rather than rebuilding the research from zero. Decision support, not a substitute for your own underwriting diligence.

Why isn't this just public data anyone could scrape?

Worth naming the real competitive landscape rather than pretending this space is empty — and being honest about what's actually hard to copy here and what isn't.

ISO / Verisk Broad industry-standard catastrophe and loss-cost data, trusted but typically a black box on methodology. Every figure is tagged VERIFIED or REPORTED inline, and corrections are kept visible rather than silently fixed.

The underlying public records — court dockets, state statutes — aren't what makes this hard to copy; any competitor can pull the same filings. What isn't public is the validated crosswalk connecting a specific statute to a specific chemical variant to a specific NAICS code to a specific policy trigger: that mapping gets more accurate and harder to replicate every week it keeps running, which is the actual bet here. This isn't a claim to replace either category above. It's a different one: that a narrow, radically transparent tool updated weekly beats a broad platform updated quarterly, for the specific accounts where it actually matters.
