Why we built Nubuye

Most of the data that would catch a casualty claim early already exists — it's just scattered across dozens of separate government databases, each with its own quirks, that nobody has time to check by hand for every submission, claim, or renewal.

Nubuye reads that data directly, live, and gives a straight answer: flagged or not, with the real source attached. No dashboard to learn, no report to interpret — just the check you actually needed.

How we work

Every number on this site traces back to a real, named government source — CPSC, openFDA, NHTSA, EPA, OSHA, USAspending, CMS, and NFIRS among them. We don't estimate or infer; if a data source has a real gap or limitation, we say so on the page rather than paper over it.

What this does and doesn't cover

Most of what's here runs on US federal data, and is built for the US casualty book specifically (ChangeSignal's real-case validation is California workers' comp case law, also US-only). We checked live whether these products translate to the UK and EU: most of the underlying agencies (HSE, OPSS, DVSA's recall-write API) either publish no API or no bulk data at all, and the UK has no statutory firefighter cancer presumption law, so the Firefighter Pricing Engine's core legal mechanism simply doesn't exist there to price against. One real UK addition came out of that check — a Fire & Rescue Authority injury-rate benchmark built on official MHCLG statistics — but it's a different, more modest tool than its US counterpart, not a port of it. If you write internationally and most of this looks US-specific, we'd rather tell you that plainly than imply coverage that isn't there.
