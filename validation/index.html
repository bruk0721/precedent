Geographic correlation — real, not causal

Do tracked contamination sites and cancer incidence line up geographically?

Two real, independent datasets, joined only by state: this project's own tracked PFAS contamination sites (state-reported) and CDC WONDER's own United States Cancer Statistics Incidence database (D205, 1999-2022, all invasive sites combined). This is a correlation table, not a causation claim — a state with more tracked contamination sites and a high cancer-case count did not necessarily get that case count FROM those sites; larger, more populous states will show more contamination sites AND more cancer cases simply by being larger. Raw case counts are shown, not age-adjusted rates, specifically so this isn't dressed up as more rigorous than it is.

Aggregate loss simulation

What's the actual exceedance curve?

The real deliverable casualty-cat and property-cat models exist to produce: not "this is risky," but "at the 1-in-20 year level, here's the aggregate loss." Built honestly, in two separate real/assumed pieces: the severity distribution is real — bootstrap-resampled directly from this project's own sourced severity-anchor dollar figures, nothing invented. The claim count is a scenario you choose, explicitly labeled as your assumption, not a frequency this project claims to predict.

What this does and doesn't prove

This shows the scientific-literature component of the Emergence Signal would have been directionally elevated years before PFAS's major settlement wave, and that it correctly separates an accelerating signal (microplastics, 6PPD-quinone) from a flat one (bisphenol A) without being told which is which. It does not prove legal causation, does not predict dollar amounts, and the 6PPD-quinone growth rate in particular is computed from a very small base (roughly 20 publications/year three years ago) — directionally real, but statistically noisier than a rate computed from a larger sample. The loss simulation above is real on its severity side and explicitly assumption-driven on its frequency side — it is a scenario tool, not a frequency prediction. None of this replaces an actuary's own judgment; it's one real, falsifiable input, shown with its own limits stated. See Model risk & limitations for the complete list.

Every number above comes from a real pipeline (momentum_backtest_pipeline.py, discovery_scan_pipeline.py) run against PubMed's own public E-utilities API, exported from the same snapshot as the rest of this project.
