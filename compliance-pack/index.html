Two specific, named pain points

RDS Liability support, and litigation funding nobody's tracking.

Two specific, named casualty problems — not general liability talk — each checked for real competition and found to have little to none. Catastrophe scenario support: liability syndicates must submit an internally-modelled scenario with no industry-provided number to start from, unlike windstorm or earthquake. Litigation funding disclosure: 83.3% of insurers admit they have no process for tracking it at all.

RDS Liability scenario support

The market's own real Realistic Disaster Scenario specification (fetched and read directly, not summarized secondhand) requires two internally-modelled liability scenarios per syndicate — it gives example categories, not numbers. The real example that matches this project's own data almost exactly, quoted directly: "An agent managing a syndicate with multiple peak exposures... would capture the cumulative effect of a number of vertical spikes" (non-professional lines, s.22.2.2, "Multiple Public/Products Losses"). This generates that scenario from real, sourced severity data already on file.

Third-party litigation funding (TPLF) disclosure monitor

A 2026 CLM industry survey found 83.3% of respondents have no established process for identifying which of their own files involve third-party litigation funding. Real state laws (Georgia, Mississippi, Tennessee) requiring disclosure have all been enacted since the start of 2025. A targeted search for competing vendors in this exact space found none.
