Your book of business

One account per line: account name, state code, NAICS code, policy limit (optional), MGA/program name (optional) (e.g. Acme Specialty Chemical, OH, 325998, 5000000, Riverside Binding Authority). The policy limit unlocks the portfolio accumulation view; the MGA/program name unlocks the delegated-authority aggregation view — both optional, you still get the ranked, flagged-account list without either.

Nothing is uploaded anywhere — this runs entirely client-side against the same real pipeline data behind the Underwriter Console. No signal found for an account is not the same as no risk; it means nothing has been collected for that NAICS/state yet. Your last pasted book is saved in this browser only, so it's here next time you open this page — clear it by clearing the textarea and re-scanning, or by clearing this site's browser storage.

Portfolio accumulation

▸

The real pattern behind large unexpected reserve charges isn't usually one bad account — it's PFAS exposure scattered invisibly across many accounts in the same book, concentrated by state or NAICS without anyone seeing the total until it's already a loss. This rolls up policy limits across every RED/AMBER account, so that total is visible now.

Delegated-authority aggregation

▸

Lloyd's itself has reported weak oversight of parts of its delegated book contributing to real loss-ratio deterioration — MGA/program growth outpacing the data to track aggregate exposure across the chain. This rolls up concentrated (RED/AMBER) limits by the MGA/program name in column 5, so an aggregation nobody's underwriting a single account can see is visible at the delegated-authority level instead.

Every figure here comes from a real pipeline run against CourtListener, SEC EDGAR, or LegiScan — the same data behind Precedent's scenario trackers and the Underwriter Console, queried live in your browser against a snapshot exported from the underlying database.
